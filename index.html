01About

I am an Assistant Professor in Business Economics at Africa Business School, UM6P, in Rabat, Morocco. My research focuses on applications of game theory, in particular mechanism design, auctions and information economics, with recent work on the economics of blockchain consensus and privacy.

Before joining UM6P in 2023, I was a tenured Lecturer in Economics at the University of Liverpool Management School, a Senior Research Fellow in Martin Hellwig’s group at the Max Planck Institute for Research on Collective Goods in Bonn, and a Visiting Professor at the Cowles Foundation, Yale University. I earned my Ph.D. at the Toulouse School of Economics under the supervision of David Martimort, and hold degrees from the New Economic School and Moscow State University. I received my University Habilitation in 2024.

Alongside theory, I have run field and neuroeconomic experiments on bidding and bargaining, studied how ballot design shapes politicians’ positions, and now co-supervise doctoral work on climate risk and financial systems.

WINE 2025 · Lecture Notes in Computer Science2026

Selling Privacy in Blockchain Transactions

with Georgios Chionas, Piotr Krysta and Rida Laraki
Published versionarXiv
Abstract

We study methods to enhance statistical privacy in blockchain transactions. We analyze economic mechanisms for privacy-aware transaction owners whose utility depends not only on the outcome of the mechanism but also negatively on the exposure of their economic preferences. First, we consider an order flow auction, where a user auctions off to specialized agents, called searchers, the right to execute her transaction while maintaining a degree of privacy, and characterize the optimal auction, which is a sealed-bid auction. We then analyze a variant of a Dutch auction in which the user gradually decreases the price and the degree of privacy until the transaction is sold. Finally, we introduce a two-sided privacy marketplace and propose a posted-price mechanism that guarantees a constant approximation of optimal social welfare while maintaining incentive compatibility on both sides and budget balance.

Games and Economic Behavior2025

BAR Nash Equilibrium and Application to Blockchain Design

with Rida Laraki and Maxime Reynouard
Published versionAAMAS 2024 extended abstractarXiv
Abstract

This paper presents a novel solution concept, BAR Nash Equilibrium (BARNE), and applies it to the Verifier’s Dilemma, a fundamental problem in the design of blockchain consensus. BARNE extends Nash equilibrium to interactions among Byzantine, altruistic and rational agents, known as the BAR setting in distributed computing. We prove existence of BARNE in a broad class of games and introduce two refinements: global and local stability. In the classical quorum-based blockchain protocol, honestly following the prescribed strategy is not a locally stable BARNE, whereas free-riding is a globally stable BARNE. We design a more robust protocol with the same classical guarantees by incorporating fines and forced errors; under it, honesty becomes the unique globally stable BARNE and free-riding is never a BARNE.

Keywords: BAR model, verifier’s dilemma, quorum-based consensus, blockchain design, stability of equilibria.

PLoS ONE2023

Economic Value in the Brain: A Meta-Analysis of Willingness-to-Pay Using the Becker-DeGroot-Marschak Auction

with A. Newton-Fenner, D. Hewitt, J. Henderson, H. Roberts, T. Mari, Y. Gu et al.
Open access
Abstract

Using the Becker-DeGroot-Marschak auction, an incentivised demand-revealing mechanism that measures subjective value as willingness-to-pay, a coordinate-based activation likelihood estimation meta-analysis of twenty-four fMRI studies (731 participants) identifies the core brain valuation system: willingness-to-pay correlates with activation in the left ventromedial prefrontal cortex extending into the anterior cingulate, bilateral ventral striatum, right dorsolateral prefrontal cortex, right inferior frontal gyrus and right anterior insula. Concurrent scanning preferentially engages mentalizing-related structures.

Psychophysiology2023

A Comparison of Reward Processing during Becker-DeGroot-Marschak and Vickrey Auctions: An ERP Study

with A. Newton-Fenner, D. Hewitt, J. Henderson, N. Fallon, Y. Gu et al.
Published version
Abstract

Vickrey and Becker-DeGroot-Marschak auctions are strategically equivalent demand-revealing mechanisms, differing only in whether the opponent is a human or a random number generator, yet behaviour differs between them. Comparing the neural correlates of outcome feedback with EEG in twenty-eight participants, we find an enhanced cortical response to bid outcomes in the Vickrey auction and face-sensitive potentials that appear only there, suggesting that the social, competitive aspect of an auction modulates how bid outcomes are processed as early as 176 ms.

Social Choice and Welfare2022

The Theory of Straight Ticket Voting

with Ioanna Grypari and Erin Hengel
Published versionPDF (open access)The Master Lever explained
Abstract

This paper explores the effects of the straight-ticket voting option (STVO) on the positions of politicians. STVO, present in some U.S. states, allows voters to select one party for all partisan elections listed on the ballot. We build a model of pre-election competition: STVO results in greater party loyalty of candidates while increasing the weight of non-partisan voters’ positions in candidate selection. This induces an asymmetric effect on winning probabilities and implemented policies in the two-party system.

Keywords: ballot design, elections, political positions.

Psychophysiology2022

Bid Outcome Processing in Vickrey Auctions: An ERP Study

with A. Newton-Fenner, J. Tyson-Carr, H. Roberts et al.
Published version
Abstract

The first EEG study of outcome processing in Vickrey auctions. Twenty-eight participants bid on household items against an anonymous computerised opponent. Bid outcomes elicit a feedback-related negativity that distinguishes relatively advantageous wins (bargains) from less advantageous ones (snatches), and a P300 that is sensitive to winning and to retail price. Individual loss aversion does not correlate with either component.

Journal of Economic Behavior & Organization2021

Selling “Money” on eBay: A Field Study of Surplus Division

with Alia Gizatulina
Published versionAccepted manuscriptAEA RCT registry
Abstract

We study the division of trade surplus in a competitive market by running a natural field experiment on German eBay. Acting as a seller, we offer Amazon gift cards with face values of up to 500 euro; randomly arriving buyers make price offers under eBay rules. Using a novel decomposition method, we find that the average share of surplus offered to the seller is 29%. We also document insignificant effects of stake size, poor use of strategically relevant public information, and behavioural differences between East and West German buyers.

Keywords: field experiment, ultimatum game, surplus division, bargaining, internet trade.

Journal of Institutional and Theoretical Economics2018

Information Aggregation through Stock Prices and the Cost of Capital

with Wolfgang Kuhle
Published versionWorking paper
Abstract

This paper studies a firm’s optimal capital structure when its stock price serves as a public signal of creditworthiness. Equity investors choose how much information to acquire privately, which creates a positive relation between the amount of equity issued and the precision of the stock price signal. Through its capital structure the firm can internalise the informational externality that stock prices exert on bond yields, so firms with strong fundamentals issue more equity and less debt than they would without the spillover.

International Journal of Game Theory2018

The Expected Externality Mechanism in a Level-k Environment

sole author
Published version
Abstract

Mechanism design relies on Nash equilibrium, yet experimental evidence suggests that people reason only a finite number of steps. I ask whether the expected externality mechanism of d’Aspremont and Gérard-Varet (1979) keeps its properties under finite-iteration thinking. Efficient implementation fails under some conditions, but the results vindicate the mechanism in the convex quasi-linear environment with finitely rational agents.

Keywords: expected externality mechanism, level-k model, bounded rationality.

Gender and the Time Cost of Peer Review

with Diane Alexander, Erin Hengel and Richard S. J. Tol
PDFTinbergen Institute DP 2023-044
Abstract

Using detailed administrative data from an economics field journal, we find that referees spend longer reviewing female-authored papers and are slower to recommend accepting them, that manuscripts by women go through more rounds of review, and that their authors spend longer revising them. Less disaggregated data from 32 economics and finance journals corroborate these results. All gender gaps decline, and eventually disappear, as the same referee reviews more papers, which suggests that novice referees initially statistically discriminate against female authors and do so less as their information about and confidence in the process improve.

One Strike and You’re Out: The Effects of the Master Lever on Senator Positions

with Ioanna Grypari and Erin Hengel
Latest versionMPI Preprint 2016The Master Lever explained
Abstract

We investigate the impact of a straight-ticket voting option, the Master Lever, on U.S. senators’ roll-call voting records. Using a difference-in-differences framework, we find that the Master Lever leads to a 3 to 6 percent rightward shift in senators’ policy positions, driven largely by the Republican party. Our findings suggest that ballot design has a non-negligible impact on policy-making, and that electoral outcomes in moderate to right-leaning Master Lever states may be especially vulnerable to right-wing, non-partisan voters.

University of Liverpool Working Paper2018

How Do Households Allocate Risk?

with Christoph Engel and Alexandra Fedorets
PDF
Abstract

Individuals often decide how much risk to impose on others. We run an experiment in the German Socio-Economic Panel with two members from each of 494 households. Participants estimate each other’s risk preferences well even when not told them, yet do not simply match those preferences when deciding on the other’s behalf: they shy away from exposing others to risk. We identify four types of individuals and two types of households.

University of Liverpool Working Paper2018

Collusion via Information Sharing and Optimal Auctions

sole author
PDF
Abstract

Building on Aumann’s (1976) description of knowledge, this paper defines robustness of auction mechanisms to collusion via information sharing as the impossibility of an agreement to collude: a cartel can agree on a contract only if it is common knowledge within the cartel that the contract is incentive compatible and individually rational. Robust mechanisms are characterised when some, all or no bidders face limited liability, and the characterisation is used to design a mechanism that is both optimal and collusion-proof.

Max Planck Institute Preprint2014

Estimate Based Dynamic Implementation

sole author
Abstract

A virtually efficient mechanism for sequentially arriving agents who hold informative signals about future type distributions. The principal organises betting on future type reports; an agent’s reward depends on how well the prior updated on his report predicts next-period reports. The mechanism satisfies participation constraints and runs no deficit after any history.

Max Planck Institute Preprint2014

Bidder Collusion and the Auction with Target Bids

sole author
Abstract

I study collusion in one-shot auctions where a buyer can bribe competitors into lowering their bids, and modify the single-unit Vickrey auction to incite deviations from the designated-winner scenario. The mechanism is entirely detail-free: it does not require knowledge of the colluders’ identities or value distributions.

Hausdorff Institute for Mathematics Preprint2010

Precluding Collusion in the Vickrey Auction

sole author
SSRNHIM preprint
Abstract

An optional assignment rule in a sealed-bid Vickrey auction, never applied in equilibrium, serves as a credible threat against any collusive agreement aimed at generating extra surplus, precluding strong and tacit subgroup collusion when cartels can commit to transfers but not to reallocation.
